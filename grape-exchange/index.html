Prior art & the idea

From grape swap to the digital bourse.

Raisin's board today is a grape swap: a list of who has what, read top to bottom, acted on by phone. It records supply. It doesn't price it, sort it, or close the loop.

A spot market is the next thing up. A perishable good trades for delivery now, at a price set by whatever is actually available this week. Supply and demand meet while the fruit is still on the vine. A harvest is a spot market by nature: the grapes exist, they won't keep, they trade now or they rot.

There is a European name for the place this could become. The bourse began in 13th-century Bruges, in the house of the Van der Beurze family, where merchants from Genoa to the Hanseatic ports gathered to price and trade goods on the spot; every commodity exchange since traces back to it. Closer to the vines, each wine town kept its Weinmarkt, the square where growers and buyers met at harvest to agree a price. This is the digital version of that square: one place the natural-grape trade goes, every September, to find each other and settle a price.

Grape swap today
  • One static list of listings
  • You read all of it to find your two
  • Contact printed in the open
  • Stays up until a human removes it
  • Records supply, nothing more
Spot market this build
  • Filtered by grape, region, volume, certification
  • The board surfaces your matches
  • Contact gated; buyers stay anonymous
  • Closes itself on the harvest clock
  • Prices and matches both sides

Three markets that already made this jump, and what each one proves for owning the category.

01

Rooser

rooser.euPerishable spot market · seafood

Fish plants upload the day's catch and the price they want; buyers filter by species, catch area, method, delivery window and a quality score. A new listing auto-messages the plant's existing buyers with a link. It took a trade that ran on phone calls and gave it a live price, without removing the humans.

Steal: the day-fresh listing tied to a clock, and the platform pushing a match to the other side instead of waiting for a browse. Leave the 3% fee and the logistics; grapes are negotiated, not boxed.

Visit rooser.eu ↗
02

Farm Tender

farmtender.com.auAg classifieds · Australia

Hay, grain and livestock, with "for sale" and "wanted" side by side, filtered by state and district. Over 86,000 buyers and sellers. No escrow, no payment on the platform: growers find each other and settle between themselves.

Steal: the proof that a no-payment board can become the default marketplace for a whole professional category. The for-sale / wanted symmetry and the regional drill-down are the baseline UX.

Visit farmtender.com.au ↗
03

Open Food Network

openfoodnetwork.orgProducer-to-buyer · open source

Built for food's awkward units and radically transparent about who grew what; a buyer sees the farmer even when they order through a hub. Free and open source, owned by the people who use it.

Steal: provenance as part of the listing, the grower's farming and certification shown up front. And the open-source route, if this should be something SuperStories, NWFA and Raisin own rather than rent.

Visit openfoodnetwork.org ↗
