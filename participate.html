§ 01 — what this is

a two-day festival, a curated floor, and an audience that already knows the difference between a wine that's alive and a wine that's been engineered to taste expensive.

de hallen, amsterdam, 28 february & 1 march '27. sunday is for consumers and professionals; monday is a quiet trade-and-horeca day. an early professionals-only hour opens each day, and a winemakers' dinner closes sunday night.

we keep the lineup curated and the pace human. no mega-fair pace, no two-minute pours per table. you stand with your wines and you have real conversations. around a thousand visitors come specifically for this.

§ 02 — what we look for

simple, stubborn,
bare.

01
organic at minimum
certified or not, but no herbicides, no synthetic pesticides, no shortcuts in the vineyard.
02
indigenous ferments
wild yeasts, slow ferments. no acidification, no chaptalization, no artificial adjustments.
03
low / no added so₂
tiny doses at bottling are fine. industrial dosing isn't where we are. zero-zero wines get a special label.
04
light hand in the cellar
no or only light filtering and fining, always vegan. the wine should reflect the ground it came from.
05
distributed in nl, or open to it
if there's no importer yet, that's fine. the festival is often where that conversation starts.
06
a story you can tell
we want growers who stand at the table and talk about their soils, not a brand rep.

§ 06 — questions we get

before you apply.

i don't have a dutch importer yet — can i still apply?

yes. a good part of the lineup each edition is without nl distribution at the time of the festival. the festival is often where that conversation starts.

can i really earn the fee back?

we keep participation fees low and let you sell bottles directly from your table. bring your own mobile pos and you can earn back part, sometimes all, of what you paid.

how many bottles should i bring?

plan for around 50-70 bottles across your wines, plus a few for the dinner. we send a detailed brief once you're confirmed.

can a distributor or rep pour for me?

preferably not. we built this around meeting the people who grow the grapes. if you can't travel, talk to us and we look at it case by case.

what about transparency on farming and sulphur?

non-distributed winemakers are asked for a chemical analysis. sulphur levels and farming practice are published on your profile, so visitors taste with their eyes open.
